The South Indian filter — two steel cups, a perforated disc, a plunger — makes a
concentrate (decoction) you cut with hot milk and sugar. It's one of the great
coffee rituals, and it works beautifully with single-estate beans, not just the
traditional chicory blend.

## Pick the right coffee

You want sweetness and body more than delicate aromatics — the milk will bury
anything too subtle. From our menu:

- **Chikmagalur Attikan Estate Honey** — jaggery and caramel, silky body. The
  natural choice; it's grown 200km from where you're probably drinking it.
- **Dawn Patrol** — built for milk, consistent, easy.
- **Midnight Runner** — if you like your filter coffee strong and dark.

Skip the light, high-acidity lots (Yirgacheffe, Huila) here — save those for
pour-over.

## Grind

Medium-fine — finer than pour-over, coarser than espresso. Too fine and the
decoction drips for 20 minutes or clogs; too coarse and it runs through weak.
Choose **South Indian Filter** grind at checkout and we'll set it for you.

## Method

1. **Dose:** 3–4 heaped tablespoons (about 20g) in the top cup for a standard
   two-serve decoction.
2. **Level and press** gently with the disc. Don't tamp hard.
3. **First pour:** a little just-off-boil water (about 90°C) to wet the grounds.
   Wait 30 seconds — this bloom matters.
4. **Fill** the top cup, put the lid on, and leave it. 15–25 minutes for a full
   drip.
5. **Serve:** roughly 1 part decoction to 3–4 parts hot milk. Sugar to taste.
   Pull it between two tumblers a few times for froth.

## Troubleshooting

- **Bitter / harsh:** water too hot, or grind too fine. Cool the water, coarsen up.
- **Weak and sour:** grind too coarse, or dose too low. Add a spoon, grind finer.
- **Drips forever:** grind too fine or pressed too hard. Ease off the disc.
