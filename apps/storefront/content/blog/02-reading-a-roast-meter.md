Every coffee we sell carries three scores out of five: **acidity**, **body** and
**sweetness**. They're not quality ratings — a 2 isn't worse than a 5. They're a
shorthand for what the coffee will feel like. Here's how to read them.

## Acidity

Acidity is the bright, lively quality — think of the snap in an apple or a squeeze
of citrus. In coffee it's a good thing when it's clean and balanced.

- **High (4–5):** Ethiopia Yirgacheffe, Colombia Huila Pink Bourbon. Sparkling,
  fruit-forward, "juicy". Best as pour-over or filter.
- **Low (1–2):** Midnight Runner Espresso, Glacier Steep Cold Brew. Smooth,
  rounded, no tang. Better with milk, or over ice.

If bright coffee tastes sour to you, that's usually a brewing issue, not the
coffee — see our [pour-over guide](/blog/pour-over-at-home-v60).

## Body

Body is weight and texture — how the coffee sits on your tongue. Skim milk versus
whole milk.

- **Light (2):** Yirgacheffe. Tea-like, delicate.
- **Heavy (5):** Sumatra Kerinci, Midnight Runner, Glacier Steep. Syrupy, coats
  the mouth, lingers.

Heavier body stands up to milk and to cold. Lighter body shows off subtle
aromatics but can feel thin with milk.

## Sweetness

Perceived sweetness — not added sugar. It's how much the coffee reminds you of
caramel, honey, ripe fruit or chocolate.

- Our honey-process Indian lots (Attikan, Araku) score 5: jaggery, ripe fruit,
  wild honey.
- Even our darkest roast, Midnight Runner, scores 3 — we roast to caramelise
  sugars, not burn them.

Higher sweetness makes a coffee more forgiving. It's the score to lean on if
you're buying for someone who says they "don't really like coffee".

## Shopping with the meters

- **Want a morning-with-milk coffee?** Low acidity, high body, mid-to-high
  sweetness. → Dawn Patrol, Midnight Runner.
- **Want something to brew black and pay attention to?** High acidity, lighter
  body, high sweetness. → Yirgacheffe, Huila Pink Bourbon.
- **Buying a gift and not sure?** Highest sweetness score you can find. → Attikan
  Estate Honey.
