Pour-over gets treated as fussy. It doesn't have to be. Here's a repeatable
recipe that works, plus the two adjustments that fix 90% of bad cups.

## What you need

- V60 (or any cone dripper) and filters
- Scale that reads to 1g
- Gooseneck kettle if you have one; a steady hand if you don't
- **15g coffee to 250g water** — a 1:16.6 ratio, easy to scale

## Which coffee

Pour-over is where light, high-acidity single origins shine:

- **Ethiopia Yirgacheffe Gedeb** — jasmine, bergamot, peach
- **Colombia Huila Pink Bourbon** — pink guava, papaya, cane sugar

Grind medium — like coarse sea salt. Choose **Pour Over** grind at checkout.

## Water

Just off the boil, 94–96°C for light roasts. Filtered water if your tap is hard.

## The pour schedule (total time ~2:45–3:15)

1. **Rinse the filter** with hot water, tip that water out. Add coffee, tare.
2. **Bloom (0:00–0:45):** pour 45g water, just enough to wet all the grounds.
   Swirl gently. The bed will puff up — that's CO₂ leaving.
3. **First pour (0:45–1:15):** pour steadily to 150g total, in slow circles.
4. **Second pour (1:30–1:50):** pour to 250g total.
5. **Drawdown:** give it a gentle swirl. It should finish draining by 3:00–3:15.

## The two adjustments

**Tastes sour, thin, sharp?** Under-extracted. Grind finer, or pour slower so the
total time stretches toward 3:30.

**Tastes bitter, dry, hollow?** Over-extracted. Grind coarser, or speed the pours
up.

Change one thing at a time. Fresh coffee (roasted in the last month) makes all of
this easier — stale coffee barely blooms and tastes flat no matter what you do.
