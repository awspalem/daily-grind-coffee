Cold brew is the most forgiving way to make coffee. No temperature to hit, no
timing to nail. If you can wait overnight, you can make it.

## Why it tastes different

Brewing with cold water pulls out sugars and low-bitterness compounds but leaves
most of the sharp acids and harsh notes behind. The result is smooth, sweet and
low-acid — easy on a sensitive stomach, great over ice.

## Which coffee

You want a coffee built for slow, cold extraction — sweet, chocolatey, heavy.

## The recipe (makes ~1L of concentrate)

- **100g coarse-ground coffee** to **800g cold filtered water** (1:8)
- Stir so all the grounds are wet
- Cover, leave on the counter or in the fridge **16–18 hours**
- Strain through a fine mesh, then again through a paper filter or cloth
- Keeps in the fridge **up to 2 weeks**

No special gear needed — a large jar and a sieve lined with a coffee filter is
fine. A French press works too: steep, then press.

## Drinking it

The concentrate is strong. Dilute to taste:

- **Black over ice:** 1 part concentrate to 1–1.5 parts cold water
- **Iced latte that isn't watery:** 1 part concentrate to 1.5 parts cold milk,
  poured over ice. Skip the water entirely.
- **Sweeten while warm:** if you want sugar or syrup, mix it into a splash of
  warm water first so it dissolves.

## Common mistakes

- **Grind too fine** → sludgy, over-extracted, hard to strain. Go coarse.
- **Steeping too long** (>24h) → woody, bitter. 16–18 hours is the sweet spot.
- **Using stale coffee** → flat. Cold brew hides a lot, but not that.
