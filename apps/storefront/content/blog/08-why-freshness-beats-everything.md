Most coffee on a shelf carries a "best before" date a year out. That date tells
you when the bag was packed, not when the coffee was roasted — and for flavour,
roast date is the only one that matters.

## Coffee is fresh produce

Roasting drives chemical reactions that keep going after the beans leave the
drum. Two things happen as a bag ages:

1. **It degasses.** Fresh coffee is full of CO₂. That's why a fresh bag puffs up
   and why fresh grounds bloom vigorously. Good — but in the first few days
   there's *so* much gas it can make espresso gush and pour-over uneven.
2. **It oxidises.** Aromatic oils react with air and stale. The bright,
   distinctive notes fade first; what's left is flat and cardboard-y. This is
   slow at first, then fast.

## The window

- **Days 0–5:** resting. Great for filter and cold brew; espresso may need a day
  or two more.
- **Days 5–30:** the sweet spot. This is what we aim to put in your hands.
- **Days 30–60:** still perfectly drinkable, losing its edges.
- **Past 60 days:** you're mostly tasting "coffee", not *this* coffee.

Whole beans age far slower than ground coffee. Ground coffee starts losing
aromatics within minutes and is noticeably tired within a week. If you can grind
at home, do — it's a bigger upgrade than a better bag.

## What we do about it

- We roast in small batches to order, not to stock a warehouse.
- Every bag is stamped with the day it was roasted.
- Bags are one-way valved and nitrogen-flushed so degassing can escape without
  air getting in.
- A subscription is timed so your next bag arrives around when you finish the
  last — fresh, on a loop.

## What you can do

- Buy a size you'll finish in 3–4 weeks (that's why we sell 250g).
- Keep it sealed, cool, dark and dry. Not the fridge, not the freezer for daily
  use — the counter, away from the hob.
- Grind per brew if you can.
