Espresso feels intimidating because there are a lot of variables. In practice you
control three, and you change one at a time.

## The three numbers

- **Dose** — how much dry coffee goes in the basket. Pick one and keep it fixed.
  18g is a good starting point for a standard double basket.
- **Yield** — how much liquid espresso comes out. Start at **2× the dose** (36g
  for an 18g dose). This is your "1:2 ratio".
- **Time** — how long the shot takes. Aim for **25–32 seconds** from first drops.

Weigh the dose and the yield. Don't eyeball it.

## Which coffee

A dark-roasted espresso blend is the easy mode: forgiving, thick crema, sweet,
no sharp edges. Our **Midnight Runner** is roasted just into second crack —
cocoa nibs, molasses, toasted almond. On our bar it settles at 18g in, 36g out,
around 28 seconds.

Choose **Espresso** grind only if you don't have a grinder — espresso really
wants a fresh grind, so a home grinder is the single best upgrade.

## Dialing in

Pull a shot at your starting numbers and taste.

| Shot tastes… | It's… | Do this |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sour, thin, watery, runs fast (<22s) | Under-extracted | Grind **finer** |
| Bitter, dry, ashy, runs slow (>35s) | Over-extracted | Grind **coarser** |
| Balanced but weak | Fine | Lower the yield (e.g. 1:1.8) |
| Balanced but harsh | Fine | Raise the yield (e.g. 1:2.2) |

Adjust the grinder by the smallest increment it allows, pull again, taste again.
Two or three shots and you're usually there.

## Things that quietly ruin shots

- **Stale beans:** espresso is unforgiving. Use coffee roasted 7–30 days ago —
  fresher than that and it gushes from CO₂; older and it goes flat.
- **Uneven tamp:** level and firm, once. Don't polish or tap.
- **Channelling:** if crema is blonde and patchy, distribute the grounds better
  before tamping.

## Milk drinks

For a flat white or cappuccino, keep the same shot and steam milk to a
glossy, paint-like microfoam at about 60°C. Midnight Runner's cocoa notes cut
straight through milk.
