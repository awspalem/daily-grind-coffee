A subscription should mean you stop thinking about running out — not that you end
up with six bags in a cupboard. The trick is matching the cadence to how much
coffee you actually drink, and using the controls when life changes.

## Step 1: work out your rate

A rough guide per person:

| How you drink | Per day | 250g lasts | 500g lasts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One filter / pour-over | ~15g | ~16 days | ~33 days |
| Two cups | ~30g | ~8 days | ~16 days |
| Espresso, 1–2 doubles | ~20–36g | ~7–12 days | ~14–25 days |

Two people, two cups each: a 500g bag every 1–2 weeks.

## Step 2: pick the cadence

Set the delivery interval slightly *longer* than your run-rate — it's easier to
skip forward than to deal with a pile-up. If a 500g bag lasts you 16 days, start
with a 2-week cadence and lean on skip when you're ahead.

## Step 3: choose the coffee

- **Fixed favourite** — pick one coffee (Dawn Patrol is the classic here) and
  let it arrive on repeat.
- **Roaster's choice / rotating** — a different single origin each delivery, so
  you're always trying something.
- **Mix** — a house blend plus a rotating slot.

You can change any of this between deliveries.

## Step 4: use the controls

- **Pause** — going travelling? Pause the whole thing, restart when you're back.
- **Skip** — one delivery too soon? Skip it; the schedule continues after.
- **Swap** — fancy something different next time? Swap the coffee without
  touching the cadence.

All of it is self-serve in your account, and there's no lock-in or cancellation
fee.

## The upsides beyond convenience

- Loyalty points build on every delivered order, so they add up faster.
- Your coffee is timed to arrive fresh, on a loop — see
  [why that matters](/blog/why-freshness-beats-everything).
- One less recurring decision.
